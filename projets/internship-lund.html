Greenhouse Gas Flux Processing — Lund University, Sweden

Research technician internship · April–June 2025 · Department of Physical Geography and Ecosystem Science

Project context

As part of a research internship at Lund University, I contributed to field measurements and data analysis investigating how agricultural and forest management relates to climate. This case study focuses on the Python tool I developed to process carbon dioxide (CO₂) and methane (CH₄) flux measurements.

My contribution

I wrote a Python program to turn measurements stored in Excel workbooks into calculated gas fluxes, daily summaries and visualisations. The work connected field data with a repeatable analysis workflow, making it easier to compare measurements across dates and crop types.

Processing workflow

  1. Read and select measurements: the script reads measurement worksheets from an Excel workbook and processes each labelled measurement series. It uses observations recorded between 60 and 120 seconds, then calculates the flux when the series reaches 121 seconds.
  2. Estimate fluxes: NumPy fits a straight line to the CO₂ and CH₄ concentration time series. The slope is combined with the chamber volume and area, alongside the gas-law constants defined for the experiment, to calculate the flux.
  3. Summarise by date and crop: calculated values are written to a results workbook and averaged by day for the two crop categories: perennial wheat and annual wheat.
  4. Visualise the results: Matplotlib produces time-series plots of mean CO₂ and CH₄ fluxes, making the evolution of each gas and the comparison between crop types easier to inspect.

Deliverables

The workflow generates an Excel table of calculated fluxes and two figures showing mean CO₂ and CH₄ fluxes over time. Example graphs and workbook screenshots are available in the project README. The source workbooks contain the measurements and are not included in the public repository.

What I learned

This project strengthened my ability to build a scientific data-processing workflow in Python, work with Excel data using OpenPyXL, apply linear regression with NumPy and communicate results with clear plots. It also gave me practical experience connecting environmental field measurements to quantitative analysis in an international research setting.
