Greenhouse Gas Flux Processing — Lund University, Sweden

Research technician internship · April–June 2025 · Department of Physical Geography and Ecosystem Science

Project context

As part of a research project called "Perennial project", I contributed to field measurements and data analysis. This project goal was to compare two types of wheat that were planted in two different fields (perennial and annual) and show the advantages of one compared to the other in terms of impact on climate change. This case study focuses on the Python tool I developed to process carbon dioxide (CO₂) and methane (CH₄) flux measurements.


My contribution

I wrote a Python program to turn measurements stored in Excel workbooks into calculated gas fluxes, daily summaries and visualisations. The script reads each measurement series, fits the concentration curve with NumPy, computes the flux from the chamber geometry, and aggregates daily averages per crop type. It is a repeatable workflow that makes measurements easy to compare across dates and between fields. Full technical details are available in the README on GitHub (see the link below).


Results

What I learned

This project was my first experience in building a scientific data-processing program in Python. I started it of my own initiative and created it with my imagination, as it went beyond what I had learned during my training. It also gave me practical experience connecting environmental field measurements to quantitative analysis in an international research environment.
